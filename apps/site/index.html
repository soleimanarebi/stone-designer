About natural stone cladding

Stone is a natural, brittle material. It is not made under factory control, so its strength varies from bed to bed and block to block; it cannot yield to relieve a local peak of stress; and weathering, microcracking, damaged edges and the drilling of fixing holes can all reduce its strength in service. The edges and fixing points, where the stresses are highest, are where it is weakest.

BS 8298 therefore sizes the panel from tests on the actual stone, at the project thickness, finish and bedding orientation, and applies a partial material factor that grows when the data are thinner: mean rather than lower expected values, three-point rather than four-point bending, dry samples only, a single set, unknown orientation, or no freeze-thaw testing.

Summarised from BS 8298-2:2020 clauses 5.5 and 5.6 and Annex D, and BS 8298-4:2020 Annex B.

  • 3.00base partial material factor for flexure and for fixing breakout, BS 8298 Table 4
  • 4.20γm of the Portland limestone in the worked example: LEV, four-point, dry, long history, Table E.1
  • 1.5 · 2.25 · 3.0kN/m², indicative low, medium and high wind for preliminary sizing, Table D.1
  • 3 of 4fixings assumed to engage on a rainscreen frame, BS 8298-4 B.4

Why Stone Designer

A stone check is short, but it hangs on details that are easy to get wrong: which span is the governing one, how many fixings really engage, and which of seven factors the test report supports.

  • Spreadsheets

    Fast for one panel, but the Table 4 factors and the span rules get retyped for every project.

  • FE models

    Detailed stresses, but the partial factors, engagement and detailing rules are still yours to apply.

  • Hand calculations

    Clear and checkable, but slow to repeat across panel sizes, fixing positions and load cases.

  • Stone Designer

    The BS 8298 method built in, every value sourced, results as you type, and a report laid out for a checker.

What it does

  • Panel thickness

    The flexural check of Annex D: design moment over the longest distance between fixings, required section modulus and required thickness.

  • Fixing breakout

    Design load per fixing with four engaged on handset work and three of four on rainscreen frames, against the tested breakout capacity.

  • Material factor from the tests

    γm built from Table 4 for the flexural and breakout data separately, with a flag when it passes 15 and a stone specialist is needed.

  • Detailing rules

    Restraint positions, dowel sizes, lengths of return, soffit limits, loadbearing fixings, and thermal movement at the joints.

  • Working you can audit

    Each check opens to its formula with your numbers in it, and the document, clause and page each value came from.

  • A report for the file

    A PDF with title block, design data, summary, loads and combinations, a result approval table and the working.

Checked against published worked examples

The engine reproduces the BS 8298-2 Annex E and BS 8298-4 Annex C worked examples (γm = 4.20, Mf = 60 750 N·mm, W = 810 N, 202.5 N and 270 N per fixing), the Annex B thermal movement example (0.43 mm/m over 3.5 m) and two panels of a stone calculation report. Expected results were computed by a separate script, outside the engine, and each case notes where the printed figures differ and why: rounding of fd to 0.71 in the annexes, a fixing count that changes between the two Annex E steps, and a required thickness in the report that does not follow from its own section modulus.

About

Stone Designer is an independent tool for facade engineers, stone specialists and cladding contractors. It applies its authors' reading of BS 8298-2:2020 and BS 8298-4:2020, with BS 8298-1 and BS 8298-3 where they are cited, and of published fixing manufacturer guidance.

It is not produced, reviewed or endorsed by BSI or by any stone supplier or fixing manufacturer. Every design it touches must still be checked and approved by a qualified structural engineer. If something looks wrong, please say so: corrections shape what comes next.
